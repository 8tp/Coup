# Hindi (hi) glossary

Coup has no Hindi edition that I could find (November 2026: Indian shops sell
the English Indie Boards & Cards box, and no Hindi printing is listed by the
publishers), so every term below is my choice. The printed plates on the card
art stay English (`CAPTAIN`, `STEAL`); the HTML labels over them are Hindi.

Audience: young urban Indian players, who mostly play in English today. The
Hindi is conversational Devanagari that keeps the English gaming words they
already say (challenge, block, bluff, bot, room) and uses everyday Hindi where
that is what people say (बारी, सिक्का, दावा, चाल).

Confidence: **high** = I would expect any Hindi-speaking player to read it
without thinking; **medium** = natural, but a native reviewer may prefer
another word; **low** = a real judgment call, check first.

## Characters

| English | Hindi | Why | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|

| Captain | कप्तान | The ordinary Hindi word. | high |
| Ambassador | राजदूत | The ordinary Hindi word. | high |
| Contessa | कॉन्टेसा | Transliterated; no Hindi title fits, and many Indians know the name (also an old Indian car). Feminine, so verbs with it are feminine: हत्या ब्लॉक करती है. | high |
| Inquisitor | जासूस | "Spy/detective". ## Register and grammar choices

- **आप throughout**, with polite imperatives in -ें (चुनें, खेलें, करें),
  the standard of Hindi app UIs. तुम would be friendlier but reads as rude
  to some players, and mixing would be worse. Player-to-player chat lines
  (quick phrases, reactions) are written so they need no pronoun.
- **Gender of the reader and of other players is never needed.** Hindi verbs
  agree with the subject's gender in the present and in intransitive
  pasts. Every sentence about "you" or a named player uses one of:
  - the ergative with a transitive verb, which agrees with the object:
    `{actor} ने टैक्स लिया`, `आपने ... का दावा किया`;
  - a possessive or noun phrase: `आपकी जीत` (you win), `{name} की बारी`,
    `{actor} की चाल: ...`, `आपका ब्लफ़ पकड़ा गया`, `{actor} का कार्ड गया`;
  - the dative: `{name} को एक कार्ड खोना होगा`, `{name} को कार्ड चुनना है`;
  - passives: `ख़ुद को निशाना नहीं बनाया जा सकता`, `... का दावा किया जा सकता है`;
  - `... के हाथ में है` for "only X can".
  Generic role nouns (खिलाड़ी, होस्ट, बॉट, निशाना, चैलेंज करने वाला) take the
  default masculine, as Hindi does for a role with no particular person.
  Character names have fixed grammatical gender (कॉन्टेसा feminine, the
  others masculine), which is why lines with a variable `{character}` use
  `{name} के पास {character} है` (no agreement) rather than a past verb.
- **Names that arrive as params must not inflect.** Hindi masculine nouns in
  -आ change before postpositions (हत्यारा → हत्यारे का). Every character,
  action, faction and the reserve name was chosen so it reads correctly
  before का/के/की/से/में/पर.
- **Plurals.** CLDR Hindi has `one` (0 and 1) and `other`. Coin counts add
  an explicit `=0` branch (`0 सिक्के`, not `0 सिक्का`), which is what a
  Hindi speaker says. Plural forms in oblique position use the oblique
  (`2 कार्डों पर`, `# सिक्कों में`, `# हत्याओं के साथ`).
- **Punctuation.** Sentences end with the पूर्ण विराम (।). Numbers are
  Western digits.
- **Timers** read "47 सेकंड" in full; the one CSS rule in the Hindi block of
  `globals.css` lets the countdown label take its natural width.
