# Glossary: Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR)

Coup is sold in Brazil by Funbox Jogos as "Coup" (the name is not
translated), with the Reformation expansion sold as "Coup: A Reforma" /
"Coup + Reforma". The character and action names below are the ones printed
in the Funbox rulebook ("livreto de regras", which also includes the
Inquisidor variant), so they are what Brazilian players already know and
search for. Where the Funbox base rulebook has no term (factions, Treasury
Reserve, Convert, Embezzle) the choice is mine and is marked as such: I could
not find the Brazilian Reformation rulebook text online.

Confidence: **high** = printed in the Funbox rulebook; **medium** = natural
choice, widely used by Brazilian players, not checked against a printed
source; **low** = my choice, a native reviewer should confirm.

## Register and grammar choices

- **Address:** "você" throughout, the neutral standard of Brazilian
  Portuguese. Imperatives in the third-person form that goes with "você"
  ("Conteste", "Escolha", "Toque").
- **Player gender is unknown.** No adjective or participle agrees with a
  player or with the reader: "você está fora" (not "eliminado"), "Você sofreu
  um Golpe" (not "foi golpeado"), "Descobriram seu blefe" (not "foi pego"),
  "Removeram você da sala", "Você foi a primeira pessoa a sair",
  "quem contestou perde uma carta". Generic role nouns (jogador, espectador,
  alvo) keep the standard masculine generic.
- **Character names in variable slots have no article** ("alega ter Condessa",
  "bloqueia com Capitão", "não tem Duque"), because the slot can hold Duque
  (m) or Condessa (f) and the param carries an emblem. This is how players
  talk at the table ("tenho Duque"). Where the character is fixed the article
  is used ("alegue o Duque", "só a Condessa").
- **Action names in variable slots:** character actions are verbs (Taxar,
  Extorquir, Assassinar, Trocar, Investigar, Desviar, Converter), so "para
  {action} {target}" reads naturally. Where an action is the subject or
  object of a sentence the app writes "a ação {action}".
- **Plurals:** CLDR puts 0 in pt's `one` category ("0 moeda"), but Brazilian
  usage puts zero in the plural ("0 moedas"). Every count plural therefore
  has an `=0` branch with the plural form. `many` (only for compact millions)
  is never needed.
- **Numbers and figures** stay as params. Ordinals use "ª" ("a 2ª pessoa a sair").
